The Governance Deck · Facilitation

Run it as a sixty-minute session.

For an executive or board meeting. Four to ten people around one table, dealing the sections as agendas. The session ends in names and dates, or it has not happened.

What you need on the table

The one-page guide, printed per person · the deck, sorted by section · the live use-case list, however rough · one sheet for the decisions · a visible timer. Reports circulate beforehand; the session is not a read-through.

TimeMove
0:00Frame it. Two minutes: this session produces owner, evidence and review date, or it produces nothing. Decisions are made here, not presented here. The test for everything agreed today is on the desk card.
0:05Run the fifteen checks. Silently, individually, eight minutes. Compare counts only: which of the five sections is thinnest for the organisation. Disagreement about counts is itself the first finding.
0:15Place the live use cases on the tiers. Take the real list. For each: Tier 1, 2, 3 or 4 — out loud, fast, first instincts. Then ask the evidence question for each claim: where is the override rate, the sign-off record, the two-person log? A tier with no evidence drops back to Tier 3 on the spot.
0:28Argue one red line. Take the decision in the room most likely to be automated by accident — the one with deadline pressure. Draft its red line live: decision, boundary, owner, verification rule, record, review date. Defend it to the client, the regulator and the staff, in that order. Then assign the same drafting to the next two, with owners and dates.
0:42Close the vendor gap. Ten questions, quickly, for the tool most in use. Any answer that is vague or verbal becomes a written ask to the vendor, with a date — before renewal, not after.
0:50Name the decisions AI may never make. Round the table, one each, no repeats. This list, rough as it is, becomes the red line inventory's first page and the board's next paper.
0:56Review dates before adjourning. Every decision made today gets its review date in the calendar before anyone stands up. An unbooked review is a decision quietly revoked.

If the room negotiates oversight down

Ask for the evidence that would justify the lower tier. If it does not exist, the tier goes up and the evidence becomes the action. The burden sits with the move down.

If everything is "Tier 3 for now"

"For now" without a review date is Tier 3 forever. Write the date, or write the tier properly.

The one rule

Nothing leaves the room as "the committee will look at it". One name per row, or the row stays open on the record.
